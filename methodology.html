Methodology

How AegisAI reaches a verdict.

No black box: three measured inputs, weights with documented sources, and fixed decision rules.

Step 1 · Inputs

Three questions, three numbers

Risk is not one number. How easily does it go wrong, is the protection in place, and how bad is it when it does — AegisAI answers all three separately, and never lets one hide another.

Exposure — 0.5 to 10

How easily the system goes wrong: the data it touches, how independently it acts, whether outsiders can reach it, and which advanced capabilities it has. The scale is floored — prompt injection, insecure output handling, supply chain and overreliance apply to every LLM system, so no system can score zero risk.

Control maturity — 0 to 5 → gap index 0 to 10

Every applicable control is scored on a six-point maturity scale, with space to attach evidence. The gaps are normalised into a single index that is comparable across systems. Compliance, partial implementation and assessment progress are reported as three separate figures — a half-built control is never counted as a finished one.

Consequence — five checkable answers

How bad a failure would be: people affected, safety of life, regulated decision process, reversibility, financial exposure. Not "is this system important?" — a feeling — but five questions somebody can go and verify. Unanswered means not declared, which we report as unknown, never as low.

Step 2 · Weighting

Gaps are weighted, and every weight has a source

Not every gap deserves the same attention. AegisAI ranks findings so the controls most likely to matter for your systems rise to the top — using weights that each carry a documented source and rationale, and can be tuned with a full audit trail. A backtest module can validate them against your own logged incidents once you have them.

The shipped defaults are expert judgment, not empirically calibrated — and the reports say so on their face. We would rather tell you that than dress a guess up as a measurement.

Step 3 · Decision rules

Four verdicts, fixed rules, no judgment calls

Two of the four block a launch. They are separate because they are different statements — and because the fix is different.

Insufficient Evidence

A mandatory control nobody has assessed yet, or a questionnaire below 30% complete. Launch is blocked — we cannot vouch for a control we never looked at. But this is missing evidence, not a deficiency we found. The fix is to finish the assessment, not to rebuild a control that may be perfectly fine.

Go

No blocking issues identified. The verdict still ships with its full evidence trail, so it can be challenged.

Conditional

Three or more observed high-severity findings. The system may proceed only under conditions.

No-Go

We assessed a mandatory control and it failed. One is enough to block a production launch. The fix is to remediate the control.

Audit practice has always separated "we found a material deficiency" from "we could not obtain evidence". Both withhold approval; only one is an accusation. AegisAI says which one it means.

Step 4 · Prioritisation

Which system gets the budget?

A verdict tells you whether one system may launch. It does not tell a CISO where to spend. So the briefing plots two axes — how exposed a system is, and how bad its failure would be — and carries the gap index inside each cell.

Act now

High exposure and severe consequence. Fix these first.

Invest / harden

Unlikely to break — but expensive when it does. Protect the blast radius, not the probability. These are the systems a single risk score never surfaces.

Technical debt

Real gaps, bounded damage. Schedule it; don't panic.

Consequence not declared

Systems whose consequence questions are unanswered get their own bucket. They are never quietly filed under "low" — an undeclared system is an unknown system.

Consequence is classified by rule from the five declarations, not by a weighted formula — a formula would need weights, and any weight invented here would be one more uncalibrated number. Because the declarations come from the customer, consequence feeds no score, no finding, and no verdict: if a declared field could lower a risk number, the product would ship a one-click way to look safe.

On top of the verdict

Verification level and maturity estimate

OWASP AISVS level (L1–L3)

Assessments are measured against the 191 requirements of OWASP AISVS 1.0, organised in three verification levels. Reports show the level achieved and the uncovered L1 requirements — AegisAI reports against the standard, it does not certify it.

AIMA maturity estimate

From the same control assessments, AegisAI derives a per-practice-area maturity estimate aligned with the OWASP AIMA v1.0 scale — an initial estimate, not a formal AIMA self-assessment.

Weights and thresholds described here reflect the engine's current defaults; organisations can tune weights to their own risk appetite, with an audit trail.
